    4 How this works

    Source. The NTSB Public API (developer.ntsb.gov), the same database behind NTSB CAROL. A server check runs every hour and keeps US piston fixed-wing cases with an event date in this year or last (from 01-Jan-2026 on; the list started at 01-Oct-2026 and was filled back to 01-Jan-2026 on 26-Nov-2026, without pings). "Piston" means NTSB lists an airplane with a reciprocating engine. New NTSB records often have no engine or category yet; those count as piston unless the make or model is a known turbine, jet or helicopter (Boeing, Robinson, Caravan, King Air and the like), so the odd turbine can slip in until NTSB fills in the engine.

    Timing. NTSB usually lists a case several days after the accident. For example, an SR22T accident on 21-Nov-2026 (N462DL) wasn't in NTSB's data yet on 25-Nov-2026, but the FAA's preliminary notice for it was entered on 23-Nov-2026.

    Prelim entries. The same hourly check reads the FAA's Preliminary Accident and Incident Notices (notices entered in the last 10 business days, usually a day or two after the event) and keeps US accidents that look like piston airplanes (incidents left out). Until NTSB lists a case for the same N-number within 3 days of the event date, the accident shows with a PRELIM badge; then the NTSB case replaces it. The FAA notice gives the N-number, make and model, place, date and whether it was fatal, but not the number killed, injuries or damage, and the FAA says it is preliminary and subject to change. Prelim entries are left out of the key stats and the trend. A fatal prelim entry sends the fatal ping; the NTSB case for it then doesn't ping again as new.

    What a ping means. A new-accident ping comes when NTSB first lists the case, usually a few days after the event. NTSB's first details are preliminary and can change. Watching needs no profile; a watch ping (with a profile) comes when a watched case's status, report date, probable cause, injury counts or closure changes, or when a watched N-number gets a new case.

    Not yet included. Mexico and Canada.

    Privacy. Your profile holds your settings, watch list, and optional email address. Anyone who has the profile code can see and change that profile, so keep it to yourself.

    Accident information is NTSB's, preliminary until NTSB completes the investigation; nothing here adds to or interprets it. Not an official NTSB or FAA product. Pings are best-effort and can be delayed or missed; don't rely on them for anything time-critical.
