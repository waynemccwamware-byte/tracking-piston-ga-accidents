    4 How this works

    Source. The NTSB Public API (developer.ntsb.gov), the same database behind NTSB CAROL. A server check runs every hour and keeps US piston fixed-wing cases with an event date from 01-Oct-2026 on. "Piston" means NTSB lists an airplane with a reciprocating engine. New NTSB records often have no engine or category yet; those count as piston unless the make or model is a known turbine, jet or helicopter (Boeing, Robinson, Caravan, King Air and the like), so the odd turbine can slip in until NTSB fills in the engine.

    Timing. NTSB usually lists a case several days after the accident. For example, an SR22T accident on 21-Nov-2026 (N462DL, listed elsewhere) wasn't in NTSB's data yet on 25-Nov-2026. Faster sources (the FAA's next-day notices) are on the to-do list.

    What a ping means. A new-accident ping comes when NTSB first lists the case, usually a few days after the event. NTSB's first details are preliminary and can change. Watching needs no profile; a watch ping (with a profile) comes when a watched case's status, report date, probable cause, injury counts or closure changes, or when a watched N-number gets a new case.

    Not yet included. The FAA's next-day preliminary notices (faster than NTSB, but only kept for 10 business days) and Mexico and Canada.

    Privacy. Your profile holds your settings, watch list, and optional email address. Anyone who has the profile code can see and change that profile, so keep it to yourself.

    Accident information is NTSB's, preliminary until NTSB completes the investigation; nothing here adds to or interprets it. Not an official NTSB or FAA product. Pings are best-effort and can be delayed or missed; don't rely on them for anything time-critical.
